Which view measures which city?

One programme, three strands. The object is always a measurement: a street-level image, a model output, an index value — each a claim about a place, made by an instrument with assumptions.

Question Three strands Selected output Method

The question

The instrument is part of the object.

Urban measurement is moving from instruments we can inspect to instruments we mostly cannot. A vision-language model reads a street and returns a label; a rendered view replaces a photograph; an index turns both into a single number. Each step adds scale and removes a way to check what the number is made of.

The programme asks a single question in three forms. Where does a model’s answer come from — the image or the place name? Which view can legitimately stand for which construct? And what may an indicator do once it enters a decision? The order matters: attribution makes the model observable, view-conditioned validity makes the measurement comparable, and decision-level work states the range within which the number can be used.

Journal article · SubmittedSustainable Cities and Society, 2026

Seeing and Assuming

A source-attribution audit of vision-language models for urban land-use mapping from street view imagery

Linyu Xiang†, Haoran Tang†, Junnan Chen, Shengyao Tan, Yiyang Yang*† Shared first authorship. * Corresponding author.

Urban land-use inventories are a shared input to transport, infrastructure, and environmental models, and vision-language models increasingly produce them from street-view imagery and a prompt carrying the place name. Output-level accuracy summarizes source reliance as an aggregate property of the model, obscuring where the observed street moves the internal representation away from the text-only baseline. We introduce an internal source-attribution audit that measures how far the visual input shifts penultimate residual-stream states beyond that baseline. Mapped across 5,000 locations in Shenzhen, internal source reliance is spatially non-stationary: location prompts set a district-wide baseline, and within the same district and predicted class, visual displacement still clusters along the road network. The audit separates model-induced spatial priors from observed streetscape evidence.

+0.090

residual network Moran’s I

Source reliance is spatially structured. Raw network Moran’s I is +0.632; removing district means gives +0.584, then answer-class means +0.351. After both, the field still clusters at +0.090 (z = +2.73, p = 0.006), concentrated at 100–200 m network distance.

+0.140 SD

through-road effect

Urban form predicts where the image matters. Through-roads show a +0.140 SD shift relative to local streets after district and class controls (95% CI +0.088 to +0.191). POI intensity is negative (−0.026); built density attenuates to zero once POI intensity enters.

cos 0.688

Boston direction transfer

The direction transfers. On a 640-point Boston sample, an independently estimated source-attribution axis reaches cosine similarity 0.688 with the Shenzhen axis. Image-quality indicators explain 0.2% of the transferred reading; the answer partition explains 40%.

49.0%

disagreement, top residual quartile

The audit prioritizes verification. Within predicted residential areas, the highest-residual quartile disagrees with the external reference layer 49.0% of the time, versus 32.1% in the lowest quartile (OR 1.99). The internal reading gives an ordering for human checks, not another accuracy score.

Xiang, L., Tang, H., Chen, J., Tan, S., & Yang, Y. (2026). A source-attribution audit of vision-language models for urban land-use mapping from street view imagery [Manuscript submitted for publication]. Sustainable Cities and Society.

Method

The instrument is part of the object. A measurement that cannot state its own assumptions is not yet evidence.

Across the strands the method moves in one direction: from reading a model’s internal state, to testing whether that reading holds across views, to asking what an indicator may legitimately do in a decision. Each step depends on the auditability of the one before it.
