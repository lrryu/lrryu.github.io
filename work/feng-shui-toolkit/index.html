Feng Shui Toolkit

Bridging Feng Shui knowledge and landscape ecology

Location
Hong Kong / Greater China
Year
2024
Type
MLA thesis
Role
Individual thesis
Award
Gold Award (Design Research), 22nd Asian Design Awards
Recognition
Selected for the 2024 Yearbook; exhibited and defended at the 2024 Public Review.

A thesis that asks whether Feng Shui can be read as a knowledge system rather than a set of beliefs. The project reconstructs the historical accumulation of Feng Shui concepts, identifies the parts that correspond to landscape-ecology principles — patch, edge, corridor, mosaic — and translates them into a drawing-based toolkit.

The output is not a design proposal but an instrument for evaluation: a shared vocabulary through which heritage practitioners, designers, and sceptics can discuss what a Feng Shui claim is doing.

The drawings were shown as a wall installation on the 6th-floor corridor of Knowles Building, HKU, 2024–25.

The instrument, live

Seven rings, read one at a time.

The Luo Pan in the thesis is rebuilt as a reading interface: hover to pull a radial set of readings, drag any ring to turn it, and step through the layers. The instrument the argument depends on, made operable.
